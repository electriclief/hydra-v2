Phoenix

the upkeep registry · door on :8146 · a beat every 10 minutes, entries raised by their owners

Phoenix does not know what maintenance is. It is a registry that runs entries their owners register, the same way Event_Handler dispatches events and the Process Manager owns processes: the module that knows a piece of upkeep is due declares it once, and the beat finds it. An empty registry is correct on a fresh install — a node with nothing registered has nothing to do, and says so instead of inventing work.

Each entry carries both surfaces on purpose: the beat is its trigger, and the same door answers for it over HTTP. A thread-only entry would be an invisible background process (forbidden here) and a port-only entry needs a caller to remember it, which is the thing this module removes.

Receipts, not silence

Every run appends one line to data/receipts/<id>.jsonl: start, end, ms, outcome (ok · failed · cancelled · skipped · refused), detail and error. The clock in data/clock.json is what makes every mean anything across a restart; the hashes in data/hashes.json are what make ifChanged mean anything at all. A refused entry is a receipt too — a bad trigger does not disappear at 3am, it shows up as the reason it never ran.

Started by the shell as a door (Basic_App/electron/main.cjs, key phoenix): one ProcessWrapper row, visible and closeable, nothing loose on the desktop. A startup beat fires two seconds after the port binds, because a beat that only starts counting after the first interval looks dead to the first reader.

A beat that fails is told, once

When a beat runs an entry whose outcome is failed, refused or cancelled, Phoenix calls Tell Mom once with by: "phoenix" and the dedupe key phoenix.upkeep.failed — a red beat is a message, not a line in a receipt file nobody opens. Silence is correct for an idle beat: no run, nothing to say. If Tell Mom is down the beat still completes and records told: {ok:false}; a notification door being unreachable must never turn into a failed cycle.
